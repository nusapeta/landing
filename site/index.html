Routing and geocoding for Indonesia, on your own machine.

A memory-mapped router and a single-file geocoder, both built from OpenStreetMap data for the archipelago. Two engines, one compose file, no database, no external service.

Image budget for 1.0 is 32 MB target and 40 MB hard cap. The router image measured 36.8 MB on 15 November 2026: under the cap, above the target.

Graph and index data: ODbL 1.0, OpenStreetMap contributors. Where a build renumbers features, osm_type plus osm_id is the identity to use; place_id is not treated as a stable external key here.

The national graph is read with mmap, so two profiles share the same pages and each router process idles near 17 MB. The on-disk format is .graph v5; the binary is nusa-serve, written in Go.

Place

One .idx file holds the whole geocoder index at format v1, built from the national PBF in 228 s. The binary is nusa-place, written in Rust.

Added in this sprint

Sprint of 18 to 19 November 2026, merged into main and released as v0.2.1 on 20 November 2026. Every number below was measured on this machine with the release binary (commit 5962811), not taken from the public demo: Linux 7.2.15-1-omarchy x86_64, the national indonesia.graph built 19 November 2026 (1,621,222,585 B, 54.9 M nodes, 112.3 M edges) and the 386,376,512 B places.idx built from the same PBF.

Endpoints and limits added in the 18 to 19 November sprint
EndpointAddedMeasured here
/matrix (new) One Dijkstra per source fills a whole row. 1 to 5 sources, 1 to 25 targets, at most 25 pairs, max_minutes positive, at most 180. Cells report ok, too_far, snap_failed or no_route. Monas to two Jakarta targets: 10,784 m / 685.4 s and 3,835 m / 315 s. 30 ms, 133,244 expansions. A third point, -6.2,106.8166, stays no_route in the matrix while /route answers it in 4,808 m: the matrix takes one snap and one Dijkstra per source, /route retries across candidate snaps.
/isochrone (new) GeoJSON rings from a time-limited Dijkstra. minutes 1 to 60, grid 50 to 10,000 m, raised automatically past 40,000 cells, then simplified at 25 m. Monas, 5 minutes: 21.21 km2 in two rings, grid 250 m, 10 ms.
/nearest (new) Snaps a coordinate to the closest road edge without running a route search. k returns up to 32 candidates with edge and node ids. Monas: 311 m to the nearest edge, three candidates returned, 3 ms.
avoid on /route toll, ferry, unpaved, motorway, comma or pipe separated. Edges are filtered during expansion, so snapping and expansion counts move too. Monas to Depok: 10,724 m / 680.9 s, identical with avoid=toll because the fastest path takes no toll road. An unknown class returns 400.
via on /route Up to five waypoints as lat,lng pairs joined by a pipe; legs are routed in order and joined into one point list. Monas to Depok through -6.2,106.8245: 12,634 m / 898.4 s, two legs, 507 points. A waypoint at -6.2,106.8166 fails with too_far: that pin sits in the dead pocket the snap retry cannot leave.
alt on /route Up to three extra routes, each accepted only when at least 20 % of its edge length differs from the routes already accepted. Monas to Depok with alt=3 returned no alternative, and the response still carries the shortest route without an error.
Ferry stitching Cross-strait legs use measured OSM duration where a connector exists, otherwise a static table of ten crossings. ferry_time_source says which when the route crosses region files; a same-file crossing reports only ferry_m and ferry_spans. Bakauheni to Merak: 29,331 m / 5,069.6 s, of which 27,056 m is ferry, has_ferry true.
/place/suggest (new) Prefix autocomplete over the index, at least 3 characters after normalisation, 1 to 20 results, optional lat/lon bias. Shorter queries return 400. q=monas with a Monas bias returns five records in under a millisecond: four Monumen Nasional and Monas in Gorontalo Utara. q=mo returns 400 invalid_request.
/place/admin/reverse (new) Deepest administrative polygon that contains the point, walked through parent_id instead of centroid distance. Sets ambiguous when several polygons at that level match. Monas: Gambir, Jakarta Pusat, Daerah Khusus Ibukota Jakarta, ambiguous false, in 7 ms on the 386,376,512 B national index (format v2, 9,360 administrative polygons). Street reverse still never interpolates house numbers.

Route status on this branch is unchanged: the E1 gate still fails at 0.23 % median and 14.49 % at the 95th percentile, and the two-level hierarchy work stays blocked until its data prerequisite is met. The release keeps the same 15 requests per minute per IP limit.

Run it yourself

The compose bundle is nusapeta/stack. The bootstrap script downloads the published data release and checks its SHA256 before anything starts. The compressed national graph is about 552 MB. 

cp .env.example .env
scripts/bootstrap.sh
docker compose up -d --wait
scripts/e2e.sh

What this does not claim

  1. Open gate Route accuracy has not passed our internal gate. The E1 gate failed: median difference against the reference is 0.23 %, the 95th percentile is 14.49 %. The gate stays open and these numbers stay published.
  2. place_id is not a Google Place ID. It identifies an OpenStreetMap feature in this index and means nothing outside it.
  3. Coverage is Indonesia. Routes above 100 km return 422 too_far.
  4. Version 0.2.1. The data formats are versioned (.graph v5, .idx v2); the HTTP API can change before 1.0.
